# S3-invariant 3×3 schemes over F3: literature and necessary conditions

*matmul-search, attempt 010, theory stream (2026-11-23). Every numeric claim is checked by
[`scripts/check_010_theory.py`](../scripts/check_010_theory.py) (`python3 -I scripts/check_010_theory.py`, about 2 s, prints PASS/FAIL per check).*

## Summary
- **The Laderman algorithm is S3-invariant.** Burichenko (2015) showed its automorphism group is S4. That group contains an S3 that cyclically permutes the factors and transposes them.
  Over F3 we conjugated it explicitly into our standard ⟨σ, τ⟩ (verified). It has **S3 split (1,4,1,3)** and σ-split (6,5), and it is inequivalent to the 006 scheme.
  So there are at least two S3 classes at rank 23 over F3, at two different splits. The same holds mod 2, which answers the case Yang left open ("except possibly ⟨△,⊺⟩").
- **No paper we found rules out S3-invariant (or Z3-invariant) rank 22 over any field other than F2.** Over F2, Yang (2024) rules out ⟨△,⊺⟩-invariant rank ≤ 21 by SAT.
  Tomar (2026, preprint) proves rank 22 is impossible over F2 for every scheme. Over C, Burichenko only restricts the abstract automorphism group, and S3 is not excluded.
- **New necessary conditions (proved, then checked on all 6 S3 classes we have):**
  - **(N1) KKK identity:** Σ over size-6 orbits of det[sk u, sk v, sk w] = −1. Hence **a ≥ 1**: every S3-invariant scheme, of any rank, has a size-6 orbit. This kills **21 of the 60** rank-22 splits (all with a = 0).
  - **(N2) KKS bound:** 3a + b + c ≥ 6. This kills **3 more**: (1,1,0,13), (1,1,1,11), (1,2,0,10).
  - **(N3) SSK bound:** 3a + b ≥ 4 (the wedge rank of the SSK block is exactly 4, by exhaustive search). It kills nothing new.
- **36 splits remain**, all with a ∈ {1, 2, 3}, listed in §5.

## 1. Literature (A1, A2)
Sources read in full text are marked (read). The others were read from the abstract or a search summary.

**Symmetric rank-23 decompositions of ⟨3,3,3⟩**
- **Burichenko, "Symmetries of matrix multiplication algorithms I"**, [arXiv:1508.01110](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.01110) (read, §5). Theorem 1.1: Aut(Laderman) ≅ S4 and Aut(Hopcroft) ≅ S3 × Z2.
  The group is generated by four maps:
  - Φ1 = T(π23, π13, 1) and Φ2 = T(π23, 1, π23);
  - Φ3(x⊗y⊗z) = yᵗε2 ⊗ ε2xᵗ ⊗ zᵗ, which is transpose-type;
  - Φ4(x⊗y⊗z) = ε1zπ12 ⊗ π12xπ12ε1 ⊗ ε1π12yε1, which is cyclic-type, with Φ3Φ4Φ3 = Φ4⁻¹.

  Aut(L)₀ = ⟨Φ1, Φ2⟩ ≅ Z2², and the image on the factor positions is all of S3. **So ⟨Φ3, Φ4⟩ ≅ S3 is a "cyclic + transpose" symmetry of Laderman's algorithm**, twisted by sign and permutation matrices.
- **Ballard, Ikenmeyer, Landsberg, Ryder, "The geometry of rank decompositions of matrix multiplication II: 3×3 matrices"**, [arXiv:1801.00843](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00843) (read, §§2, 4, 5). They give three rank-23 decompositions with the standard Z3 symmetry:
  - S_{Z4×Z3}, with group Z4 × Z3;
  - S_{2fix-Z3}, with group exactly Z3 (Prop. 5.4, which says no transpose-like symmetry);
  - a Z3-invariant member of the Laderman family (§2.3), whose group is S4 since it lies in Laderman's family.

  In §4 they define "transpose-like" symmetries: a transpose composed with an element of PGL³. They call the ones with trivial PGL part "convenient" and remark that convenient ones "do not appear to be compatible" with the standard cyclic symmetry in known minimal decompositions.
  That fits what we see: the τ that occurs is g_M τ with M symmetric, which over F3 is conjugate to the standard τ (fact 2 of attempt 010).
  Props. 5.2, 5.3 and 5.6 say that inside each family, the standard-Z3-invariant members form one diagonal-PGL3 orbit times the standard transpose.
- **Yang, "Ruling out low-rank matrix multiplication tensor decompositions with symmetries via SAT"**, [arXiv:2402.01011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01011) (read, html). This is **over Z/2 only**.
  - Groups used: ⟨△⟩ with △(A,B,C) = (B,C,A); ⟨△, ⊺⟩ with ⊺(A,B,C) = (Cᵀ,Bᵀ,Aᵀ), which is ≅ S3; and ⟨△, ϕ_{F,F,F}⟩ ≅ C6.
  - Orbit bookkeeping: 6R_id + 3R_⊺ + 2R_△ + R_{⟨△,⊺⟩}, the same as our (a, b, c, d).
  - Results: rank ≤ 21 is ruled out for ⟨△,⊺⟩ (328,787 s) and for ⟨△,ϕ⟩ (16,779 s). For ⟨△⟩ only rank ≤ 15 is ruled out; rank 16 made no progress in more than 900,000 s.
  - It cites earlier work by Yang and Vassilevska Williams (MIT Undergraduate Research Journal 43 (2022) and 45 (2023), not read): "no subgroup of the form ⟨△,⊺,f⟩ has any rank-23 decompositions, except possibly ⟨△,⊺⟩".
    Our check that Laderman mod 2 is ⟨σ,τ⟩-invariant (§4) settles that open case: rank 23 with S3 exists over F2. Their ⊺ is a different representative from our τ, but the two groups are conjugate.
- **Burichenko, "On automorphism group of a possible short algorithm for multiplication of 3×3 matrices"**, [arXiv:2211.06485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06485) (read, §1). Over C: if an algorithm has length l ≤ 22, then Aut(P) embeds in S_l × S3 via (permutation of the terms, permutation of the factors).
  This **does not exclude S3**. The same paper reports (refs [11], [12] = [arXiv:2211.03404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03404), [arXiv:2211.04032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04032), not read) that no algorithm of length ≤ 23 is invariant under a particular group ≅ S4 × S3.
- **Moosbauer & Poole, "Flip graphs with symmetry"**, [arXiv:2502.04514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04514) (summary). They work with C3 and C3 × Z2 symmetry over F2 and lift by Hensel. For 3×3 they reach rank 23, and make no rank-22 claim.
- **Chiantini, Hauenstein, Ikenmeyer, Landsberg, Ottaviani**, [arXiv:1706.05074](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05074) (summary). They study the Z3-symmetrized tensor tr(X³) and its symmetric (Waring) rank. Our fixed-sum identity is the char-3 shadow of evaluating at X = Y = Z.
- Arai, Ichikawa, Hukushima, [arXiv:2312.16960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16960): adaptive flip graphs over F2 for 4×5 and 5×5. Nothing about 3×3 symmetry.
  Heule, Kauers, Seidl, [arXiv:1905.10192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10192): thousands of F2 rank-23 schemes. We did not check their symmetry statements.

**Lower bounds and impossibility (A2)**
- F2: Yang rules out ⟨△,⊺⟩ at rank ≤ 21 (SAT, Z3 solver). Wang, [arXiv:2609.06725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06725) (2026, abstract): R(⟨3,3,3⟩) ≥ 21 over F2.
  Tomar, [arXiv:2610.11823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11823) (2026, preprint, certificates checked in Lean 4, abstract): R = 23 over F2 (substitution method plus exhaustive search). So over F2 every rank-22 scheme is excluded, symmetric or not.
- C (char 0): only Burichenko's structural results above, plus the S4 × S3 nonexistence at ≤ 23. **We found nothing that rules out Z3- or S3-invariant rank 22 over C, Q or F3.**
  The F3 statements in `writeups/cyclic-f3.md` (T1, T2) are, as far as we found, the only results of this kind over F3.

## 2. Setup
Convention of `matmul.cyclic` and `matmul.s3`:
- A term (U, V, W) is the trilinear form ℓ_U(A) ℓ_V(B) ℓ_W(C), where ℓ_U(A) = Σ U_ij A_ij. The scheme computes P(A, B, C) = tr(A B Cᵀ).
- The symmetries are σ(U,V,W) = (V, Wᵀ, Uᵀ) and τ(U,V,W) = (Vᵀ, Uᵀ, Wᵀ).
- Orbit types (attempt 010):
  - size 6: {σᵏt, τσᵏt};
  - size 3: {s, σs, σ²s} with s = (u, uᵀ, w), w = wᵀ;
  - size 2: (x, x, xᵀ) and (xᵀ, xᵀ, x);
  - size 1: (x, x, x) with x = xᵀ.
- **Split** (a, b, c, d) counts the orbits of sizes 6, 3, 2, 1.

The characteristic is not 2, so M₃ = Sym ⊕ Skew (dimensions 6 and 3). For U ∈ M₃ and S ∈ Sym, K ∈ Skew:
- ℓ_U(S) = ℓ_{U_S}(S) and ℓ_U(K) = ℓ_{U_K}(K), where U_S and U_K are the symmetric and skew parts of U;
- ℓ_{Uᵀ}(S) = ℓ_U(S), and **ℓ_{Uᵀ}(K) = −ℓ_U(K)**;
- if w is symmetric, ℓ_w(K) = 0.

Write sk(U) = (U₁₂−U₂₁, U₁₃−U₃₁, U₂₃−U₃₂). In the basis K₁₂ = E₁₂−E₂₁, K₁₃, K₂₃ of Skew, ℓ_U(Σ k_e K_e) = sk(U)·k.

**Restriction.** Restricting the identity P = Σ_t ℓ_U⊗ℓ_V⊗ℓ_W to A ∈ X, B ∈ Y, C ∈ Z (X, Y, Z ∈ {Sym, Skew}) gives P|_{XYZ} = Σ_t (ℓ_U|X)⊗(ℓ_V|Y)⊗(ℓ_W|Z). Each term is again of rank ≤ 1.
The 8 blocks together are equivalent to the Brent equations. σ permutes the slot types cyclically, so up to σ there are 4 block types: **KKK, KKS, SSK, SSS**.

**Per-orbit contributions** (derived below; the check script verifies them on 400 random S3-shaped orbits of each type):

| block | size 6 | size 3 | size 2 | size 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| KKK | det[sk u, sk v, sk w]·ε | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| KKS, C-flattening rank | ≤ 3 | ≤ 1 | ≤ 1 | 0 |
| SSK, wedge terms (α∧β)⊗γ | 3 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| SSS | Sym(abc), 6 terms | Sym(u²w), 3 terms | −x_S⊗x_S⊗x_S | x⊗x⊗x |

## 3. Proofs
### (N1) The KKK identity: Σ over size-6 orbits of det[sk u, sk v, sk w] = −1, so a ≥ 1
*Proof.* Restrict to A, B, C ∈ Skew.

**The tensor side.** P(K, K′, K″) = tr(K K′ K″ᵀ) is alternating in the coordinates k, k′, k″, since tr(K K′ K″) on so(3) is a multiple of the triple product. Its value at (K₁₂, K₁₃, K₂₃) is computed directly:
- K₁₂K₁₃ = −E₂₃;
- −E₂₃(E₃₂ − E₂₃) = −E₂₂, whose trace is −1.

So P|_KKK = −ε, where ε is the Levi-Civita tensor in the basis K₁₂, K₁₃, K₂₃.

**The scheme side, orbit by orbit.**
- **Size 3.** Each of s = (u, uᵀ, w), σs = (uᵀ, w, uᵀ) and σ²s = (w, u, u) has the symmetric w in some slot, and ℓ_w vanishes on Skew. So the orbit contributes 0.
- **Size 2.** With ℓ = ℓ_x|Skew, the pair gives ℓ⊗ℓ⊗(−ℓ) + (−ℓ)⊗(−ℓ)⊗ℓ = 0.
- **Size 1.** x is symmetric, so the term is 0.
- **Size 6.** Put p = sk u, q = sk v, r = sk w. The six terms restrict to
  - p⊗q⊗r (from t),
  - q⊗r⊗p (from σt = (v, wᵀ, uᵀ): the two sign changes cancel),
  - r⊗p⊗q (from σ²t),
  - −q⊗p⊗r (from τt = (vᵀ, uᵀ, wᵀ): three sign changes),
  - −r⊗q⊗p and −p⊗r⊗q (from τσt and τσ²t).

  That is the alternating sum Σ_π sgn(π)·(permuted p⊗q⊗r) = det[p, q, r]·ε.

Comparing the two sides gives Σ_{size 6} det[sk u, sk v, sk w] = −1. In particular a ≥ 1. ∎

**Notes on (N1).**
- The determinant does not depend on which orbit member is used as the representative, or on its scaling:
  - (v, wᵀ, uᵀ) gives det[q, −r, −p] = det[p, q, r];
  - (vᵀ, uᵀ, wᵀ) gives det[−q, −p, −r] = det[p, q, r];
  - (εu, δv, εδw) multiplies it by ε²δ² = 1.
- Nothing in the proof uses F3 except char ≠ 2. So **every ⟨σ,τ⟩-invariant decomposition of ⟨3,3,3⟩ over any field of characteristic ≠ 2 has a size-6 orbit.**
- Checks: in all 6 known S3 classes a = 1 and det = −1 = 2.
- It is a single cubic equation on the size-6 slots, so it can be added to the CNF as a redundant constraint. For a = 1 it says the three skew parts sk u, sk v, sk w form a basis.

### (N2) The KKS bound: 3a + b + c ≥ 6
*Proof.* Restrict to A, B ∈ Skew and C ∈ Sym, and flatten along C. This gives the linear map Φ: Sym → Bil(Skew), S ↦ ((K, K′) ↦ P(K, K′, S)).

**For P,** Φ is injective. P(K, K′, S) = tr(K K′ S), and the products K K′ span a space whose symmetric part is all of Sym:
- K₁₂² = −(E₁₁+E₂₂), K₁₃² = −(E₁₁+E₃₃), K₂₃² = −(E₂₂+E₃₃). These are independent because det[[1,1,0],[1,0,1],[0,1,1]] = −2 ≠ 0.
- K₁₂K₁₃ + K₁₃K₁₂ = −(E₂₃+E₃₂), and similarly for the other two pairs.

The trace form on Sym is nondegenerate, so rank Φ_P = 6. The script confirms this.

**For the scheme,** a term contributes ℓ_W(S)·(fixed bilinear form), which has rank ≤ 1. Terms with the same C-functional on Sym add up to rank ≤ 1. On Sym, ℓ_{Wᵀ} = ℓ_W, so the C-functionals of each orbit are:
- **size 6:** w, uᵀ, vᵀ and their transposes, so at most 3 distinct functionals;
- **size 3:** only s = (u, uᵀ, w) survives, since σs and σ²s have w in a Skew slot. That is 1;
- **size 2:** xᵀ and x, which agree on Sym. That is 1;
- **size 1:** 0.

So 6 ≤ 3a + b + c. ∎

### (N3) The SSK wedge bound: 3a + b ≥ 4 (no new kills)
**Per orbit.** Restrict to A, B ∈ Sym and C ∈ Skew.
- **Size-6 orbit.** A τ-pair {t, τt} with t = (U, V, W) gives
  ℓ_U(S)ℓ_V(S′)ℓ_W(K) + ℓ_V(S)ℓ_U(S′)(−ℓ_W(K)) = (U_S ∧ V_S)⊗W_K.
  The three pairs give 3 wedge terms.
- **Size-3 orbit.** s vanishes, because its C-slot w is symmetric. σs and σ²s are swapped by τ and give one wedge term ±(u_S ∧ w)⊗u_K.
- **Size 2.** x_S x_S(−x_K) + x_S x_S x_K = 0.
- **Size 1.** 0.

**For P.** Every slice tr(S S′ K) has I in its radical. The wedge rank of P|_SSK is the least r such that the 3 slices lie in the span of r decomposable 2-forms (restricted to a complement of I). Such a span is L ⊕ Z, with L the span of the slices and Z inside a fixed complement.
The script enumerates every Z with dim Z ≤ 1: rank 3 is impossible and rank 4 is attained. So 3a + b ≥ 4.
This only matters for a = 1, where it gives b ≥ 1, which T1 (2a + b ≥ 3) already implies.

### SSS and the Frobenius identities (no new kills)
**SSS block.** A size-6 orbit gives the full symmetrization of a_S⊗b_S⊗c_S, and a size-3 orbit gives that of u_S⊗u_S⊗w. In char 3, both lie in the span of the non-diagonal symmetrized monomials. Only the fixed terms reach the diagonal e^{⊗3}, so we get the fixed-sum identity again.
The C-flattening gives only 3a + 2b + c + d ≥ 6.

**Polarized identity.** Evaluating at (X, X, Xᵀ + C) gives a further identity: the "orbit cubic" g(X) = Σ_orbits ℓ_u(X)ℓ_v(X)ℓ_w(Xᵀ) is fixed by the fixed terms, up to adding Σ c_α X_α³.
The reason is that in char 3 the only cubics with zero gradient are the Frobenius cubes, and ∇g = tr(X²·) − Σ_fixed ℓ_x(X)² ℓ_x(·).
This identity, and the trace identities obtained by contracting the KKS block, are linear consequences of the Brent equations. They give no new bound on (a, b, c, d).

## 4. Laderman is S3-invariant over F3 and over F2 (a second S3 class)
- We used Burichenko's tensor form t₁..t₂₃ (Prop. 5.1 of arXiv:1508.01110), with (U, V, W) = (x, y, zᵀ) in our convention. It verifies over Z and mod 3.
- It is invariant under Φ3 and Φ4 mod 3 and mod 2 (checked). Orbit sizes are 6, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2, 1, 1, 1.
- **Conjugation into the standard form.** Φ4 = T(I, π12, ε1) · s⁻¹ · T(I, π12, ε1)⁻¹, where s: x⊗y⊗z ↦ y⊗z⊗x is our σ. So T(I, π12, ε1)⁻¹ · Laderman is σ-invariant (checked).
  A search over the 11,232 maps g_N, which commute with σ, finds N = [[0,1,1],[0,1,2],[1,0,0]] that also makes it τ-invariant. The result is the **standard ⟨σ,τ⟩-invariant form, verified mod 3**.
- **Its invariants.** Split (1,4,1,3), σ-split (6,5), class key `c4e9a59402364eb3`.
  The 006 S3 scheme has `eebc48753638175a` and split (1,5,0,2). So the two are inequivalent, and the Laderman form is also not among the 11 cyclic classes of `results/f3/cyclic_rank23.jsonl`, all of which have split (7,2).
  Ballard et al. give a Z3-invariant member of the Laderman family with the same shape (5 fixed terms + 6 orbits). We did not compare it with their explicit matrices, so we do not claim the two are the same over F3.
- **Mod 2.** N = [[0,0,1],[0,1,0],[1,0,0]] gives a standard ⟨σ,τ⟩-invariant form of Laderman over F2.
- Running `python3 -I scripts/check_010_theory.py --save-laderman PATH` writes the F3 scheme to PATH.

**Other S3 classes present** in `results/f3/010*.json` at the time of writing, found by the other 010 streams: `45c4fc24a46604f9`, `96dc93c9112ca072`, `6d3e7c521bfd4177`, `fa7153efdb949121`. All have split (1,5,0,2).
All 6 classes satisfy the fixed-sum identity, (N1) with det = 2, and (N2), and all 8 blocks match the tensor.
The norms (k·k) of their three size-6 skew parts are (0,0,2), (0,0,2), (1,0,1), (1,0,1), (2,0,0) and (0,0,0).

## 6. Where this leaves the search (recommendation)
1. **Drop the 24 splits** killed by (N1) and (N2). The proofs are short; have them read by a second person before citing them as theorems.
2. **Add (N1) to the encoder** as one mod-3 cubic: Σ_k det[sk u_k, sk v_k, sk w_k] = 2 over the size-6 base slots. For a = 1 it forces the 6-slot to be nonzero with independent skew parts.
3. **Break the remaining symmetry using the skew parts.** The remaining symmetry g_M (M orthogonal up to sign, a group of order 24) acts on skew parts as SO(3, F3). Its orbits on nonzero vectors are the three norm classes k·k = 0, 1, 2 (sizes 8, 6, 12; checked).
   So for a = 1 the size-6 base can be pinned up to this group:
   - 3 cubes for sk u, one per norm class;
   - or, using det ≠ 0 and the free action on bases, 5616/24 = 234 cubes for the pair (sk u, sk v).

   This is far cheaper than the 65–479 orthogonal classes of matrices considered in stage A. It is a symmetry reduction (at most a factor of 24), not a pruning.
4. **Use Laderman (1,4,1,3) as a second calibration and seed.** The rank-22 splits next to it, (1,4,1,2) and (1,4,0,4), are among the 36 left. A frozen-neighbourhood theorem like T2 (cyclic-f3.md) is the natural next proof step.
5. **Further block-local bounds will not close the gap.** Each block's bound is attained in isolation: KKS needs exactly 6 terms, and the SSK wedge rank is 4. The remaining obstruction is global, mostly in the SSS block.
   The strongest route to a conclusion is SAT on the 36 splits with (N1) added and the size-6 skew-part symmetry broken, smallest instances (a = 3, then a = 2) first.
