# 010: S3-invariant schemes over F3

## Workflow
Everything is committed to `main` and pushed; there are no branches. Every run's stdout log goes to `results/logs/010-s3-f3/`, every instance gets one line in `results/runs.jsonl`,
and outcomes go in this note. **Seeds start at 100001.**

## Why
- Cyclic symmetry alone over F3 stops at 2 orbits on the proof side and at distance 2 on the local side (009, `writeups/cyclic-f3.md`).
- Over F2, adding one more symmetry took Yang from "rank ≤ 15 ruled out" (cyclic alone) to "rank ≤ 21 ruled out".
- The natural extra symmetry here is the transpose τ, which together with σ generates S3. Imposing it roughly halves the unknowns: at most 123 F3 values at rank 22, against 198 with σ alone.

## Facts established before planning (computed 2026-11-23)
1. **τ in our convention.** A term (U, V, W) contributes U[i][j]·V[j][l]·W[i][l] = tr(U V Wᵀ). The map τ: (U, V, W) → (Vᵀ, Uᵀ, Wᵀ) keeps it,
   since tr(Vᵀ Uᵀ W) = tr((UV)ᵀ W). Checked numerically:
   - τ² = id;
   - τστ = σ⁻¹ on 50 random terms;
   - τ maps BILR to a correct scheme.

   So ⟨σ, τ⟩ ≅ S3.
2. **The choice of τ is essentially unique.** Any involution τ′ with τ′στ′ = σ⁻¹ is τ′ = h·τ with h commuting with σ. Taking the commuting symmetries to be the g_M, τ′ = g_M τ.
   (h may also include powers of σ, but σᵏτ is conjugate to τ by a power of σ.) τ_M is an involution exactly when M = ±Mᵀ, and 3×3 skew-symmetric matrices are singular, so M is symmetric.
   Conjugating by g_N turns τ_M into τ_{NMNᵀ}. Over F3, a nonsingular symmetric M is congruent to I or to −I, because −1 is a nonsquare and det(−M) = −det M, and g_{−I} = g_I.
   **Up to equivalence there is one S3 containing our σ, the one generated by the standard τ.** The step "the symmetries commuting with σ are the g_M" is to be checked in stage 0.
3. **An S3-invariant rank-23 scheme over F3 exists.** Of the 11 cyclic classes, only the 006 scheme (`eebc48753638175a`) is invariant under some τ_M:
   M = [[1,0,0],[0,2,1],[0,1,0]] (and −M). After conjugating by N = [[0,1,0],[1,1,1],[1,2,2]], it is invariant under σ and the standard τ.
   It still verifies mod 3 at rank 23. **Its S3 orbits: 1 of size 6, 5 of size 3, 2 of size 1**, which is S3 split (1,5,0,2) (notation below).
   A scratch copy is in `scratch/s3_006.json`; stage 0 saves it properly. So S3 does not rule out rank 23 over F3, and a rank-22 result would be meaningful.

## The S3 ansatz (to be checked by tests in stage 0)
The orbits of S3 on the terms have size 6, 3, 2 or 1. Write the rank as 6a + 3b + 2c + d. The σ-split is (2a + b, 2c + d).
- **Size 6:** a σ-orbit of t = (u, v, w) together with the σ-orbit of τt = (vᵀ, uᵀ, wᵀ). This is 27 unknowns.
- **Size 3:** a σ-orbit that τ maps to itself. Its stabilizers are the three involutions, which are conjugate, so one member t is fixed by τ itself. τt = t as tensors means
  v = αuᵀ with αβ = 1 and γ = 1. After rescaling, **t = (u, uᵀ, w) with w = wᵀ**. This is 9 + 6 = 15 unknowns.
- **Size 2:** two σ-fixed terms (x, x, xᵀ) and (xᵀ, xᵀ, x), with x ≠ xᵀ. This is 9 unknowns.
- **Size 1:** a σ-fixed term with x = xᵀ. This is 6 unknowns.
- The fixed matrices still sum to I (σ-fact F2). In S3 terms: Σ over size-1 x, plus Σ over size-2 (x + xᵀ), equals I.

**Splits of rank 22.**
- 60 tuples (a, b, c, d) with 6a + 3b + 2c + d = 22 and 2a + b ≥ 3. The case 2a + b ≤ 2 is excluded by T1 (009).
- Every slot may be zero, so each instance covers every split below it, and the 60 instances at rank exactly 22 cover every S3-invariant scheme of rank ≤ 22.
- A size-6 slot whose t happens to be τ-fixed gives a repeated term. That only adds solutions, so UNSAT stays sound. Any SAT answer is decoded, merged and verified.
- Largest instance: 123 F3 values.

**Symmetry breaking.**
- Sign normalisation: the first nonzero entry of u is 1 in size-6 and size-3 slots, and of v as well in size-6 slots.
- Lex order within each slot type.
- For size-2 slots, x ≤ xᵀ in lex order.

The remaining global symmetry is g_M with M orthogonal up to sign (MᵀM = ±I), since only those commute with τ. It is left alone unless stage B needs it.

## Plan
- **Stage 0: code and tests (no budget needed).**
  - `matmul.s3`: `tau`, `is_s3_invariant`, and `S3F3CNF(n, a, b, c, d)`. It builds the σ-encoding with 2a + b orbit slots and 2c + d fixed slots, then ties slots together:
    - a size-6 pair: slot k′ = τ(slot k);
    - a size-3 slot: v = uᵀ and w = wᵀ;
    - a size-2 pair: x′ = xᵀ;
    - a size-1 slot: x = xᵀ.

    It replaces the σ symmetry breaking with the S3 one above. The ties are equality clauses on the value bits.
  - Save the transformed 006 scheme as `results/f3/010_3x3x3_rank23_s3_from006.json`.
  - Tests:
    - τ is a tensor symmetry and τστ = σ⁻¹;
    - fact 2's congruence claim, by enumeration: all 468 nonsingular symmetric M are congruent to ±I;
    - every symmetry h = g_M commutes with σ, and a brute-force check on 2×2 that the centralizer of σ among (A, B, C)-type symmetries is {g_M}, up to scalars;
    - the 2×2 analogue of the size-3 form: enumerate all τ-fixed rank-one tensors over F3 and check that each is (u, uᵀ, w) with w = wᵀ after rescaling;
    - the saved 006 scheme satisfies S3F3CNF(3, 1, 5, 0, 2);
    - the equation-map test of 009, reused for the S3 encoder;
    - 2×2: some S3 split of rank 7 is SAT, giving a verified S3-invariant scheme, and every S3 split of rank 6 is UNSAT.
- **Stage A: calibration (≤ 20 CPU-min, seed 100001).**
  - Rediscover rank 23 at S3 split (1,5,0,2) from scratch (cap 300 s). Check that the result verifies and is S3-invariant, and record its class key.
  - Then the four rank-22 splits closest to it, with σ-split (7,1): (3,1,0,1), (2,3,0,1), (1,5,0,1) and (0,7,0,1). Cap 120 s each, proofs for UNSAT.
- **Stage B: all 60 rank-22 splits (≤ 120 CPU-min, seed 100002).**
  - Smallest instances first. Cap 120 s, proof check 600 s.
  - Progress measure: splits closed out of 60.

## Decision rule (fixed before running)
- **A rank-22 model** that passes `matmul.verify --mod 3` and `is_s3_invariant`: stop. Save it, re-check with a second solver, compare with the catalogue, try lifting.
- **Stage A does not rediscover rank 23 in 300 s:** the S3 encoding is too slow, or broken, even where a solution is known. Debug it (the saved scheme satisfies the CNF, so it is a speed problem) before stage B.
- **All 60 splits UNSAT, every proof VERIFIED:** theorem **"no S3-invariant 3x3x3 scheme of rank ≤ 22 over F3"**, with S3 unique up to equivalence (fact 2).
  Then re-check it with Kissat as in 009, and add it to the write-up.
- **Some splits time out:** record which. For a follow-up, split them into cubes by the orthogonal-conjugacy class of one matrix, the analogue of 006's class cubes for the smaller group.
  Don't rerun with longer caps without that new idea.

## Backup
If S3 itself is too weak to help, try S3 × Z2. The extra element would be a symmetry commuting with S3, for example g_M with M orthogonal of order 2. That halves the unknowns again.
It does not cover all of S3-invariant rank 22; it gives a theorem for a smaller class, which is still progress if S3 stalls.

## Stage 0 result
- **Code.** `src/matmul/s3.py` provides:
  - `tau`, `tensor_key`, `is_s3_invariant`;
  - `s3_split` (a τ-invariant cyclic scheme → its S3 slots), `s3_normal_form`, `scheme_s3_split`, `s3_splits`;
  - `S3F3CNF(n, a, b, c, d)`. It subclasses `CyclicF3CNF` and ties slots by equality clauses (range `tie_clauses`), with its own symmetry breaking. The cyclic equations are unchanged, so 009's equation-map test still covers them.
- **The 006 scheme in S3 form** is saved as `results/f3/010_3x3x3_rank23_s3_from006.json`. It verifies mod 3, is cyclic and S3-invariant, has split (1,5,0,2) and class key `eebc48753638175a`,
  and satisfies `S3F3CNF(3,1,5,0,2)` (23,797 vars, 152,421 clauses).
- **Fact 2, the missing step, proved by hand.** Let h = (A, B, C) act by (U, V, W) → (AUB⁻¹, BVC⁻¹, A⁻ᵀWCᵀ). Then hσ = σh forces BVC⁻¹ ∝ AVB⁻¹ for all V, so A ∝ B ∝ C, and h is g_A up to a scaling of the tensor.
  A scaling that keeps the tensor is the identity. An element with a non-trivial factor permutation commutes with σ only if that permutation is in ⟨σ⟩. So every τ′ with τ′στ′ = σ⁻¹ is g_M σᵏ τ.
- **The congruence claim, checked by enumeration (test).** All 468 nonsingular symmetric 3×3 matrices over F3 have the form ±NNᵀ. So the S3 containing σ is unique up to equivalence **for 3×3**.
- **Surprise, 2×2.** All 5 S3 splits of rank 7 are UNSAT for the standard τ. That is not a bug:
  - in even dimension, −M has the same determinant class as M, so there are **two** inequivalent S3's for 2×2;
  - enumerating all 36 normalised solutions of the cyclic rank-7 encoder, none is invariant under a τ_M with det M a square (τ's class), and every hit has det M = 2.

  This is an independent cross-check of the encoder's rank-7 UNSAT, now in a test. The encoder does find S3 schemes: rank 8 is SAT for (0,1,1,3) and (0,2,0,2), and the naive scheme is S3-invariant. Rank 6 is UNSAT.
- The 2×2 τ-fixed rank-one tensors were enumerated: all have the size-3 form (u, uᵀ, w) with w = wᵀ after rescaling. That checks the parametrisation the encoder ties.
- Tests: 145 passed (8 new in `tests/test_s3.py`).

### Why not orthogonal-class cubes (checked before building them)
After S3 only conjugation by orthogonal M acts: |O(3, F3)| = 48, and g_{-M} = g_M, so the acting group has order 24 (against |GL(3, F3)| = 11,232 in 006).
Its classes, by enumeration: 926 on nonzero matrices, 479 up to sign, 65 on symmetric matrices. So a split would need 65 to 479 cubes (006 had 21), each cutting at most a factor of 24.
At 120 s per cube that is about 2 CPU-hours per split. Not built.

## Native S3 encoder
- `S3NativeF3CNF` builds the S3 ties into the variables instead of adding equality clauses. Partner slots and transposes reuse the base values, and symmetric matrices have 6 free values.
  It needs a small hook in `CyclicF3CNF` (`_make_slots`). The cyclic encoder's equation deduplication then merges equations that coincide.
- **3x3: 249 σ-equations become 138.** The CNF is half the size: (1,5,0,2) has 11,625 vars and 73,742 clauses, against 23,797 and 152,421 for the tied encoder.
- Tests (5 new, 150 pass in all):
  - the 006 scheme satisfies it, including inside a larger instance;
  - every merged equation equals the Brent residual at each of its index tuples, on random S3-shaped slots (2x2 and 3x3);
  - 2x2: SAT/UNSAT agrees with the tied encoder on every split of ranks 6, 7 and 8, and the SAT models verify and are S3-invariant.
